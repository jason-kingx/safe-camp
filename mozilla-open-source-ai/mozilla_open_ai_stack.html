The user agent is the AI interface you talk to — ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot. It sits between you and the rest of the AI stack, which means it decides what you see, what gets filtered, and whose interests are served.

Open-source user agents would be genuinely on your side: interfaces you can inspect, configure, and trust — not tools quietly optimising for the platform that built them.

Developer interfaces are the tools that let programmers build AI-powered products. Today, a single line of code hands control to OpenAI or Anthropic — their rules, their pricing, their terms, with no recourse if anything changes.

Open-source developer interfaces would be plug-and-play: a standard where you could swap in any model, any memory provider, or any guardrail, the same way cloud developers already switch between AWS and Azure without being locked in.

Data is what AI models are trained on — text, audio, and images scraped from the web, much of it taken without consent or payment.

Open-source data would mean a marketplace where provenance is tracked, creators are compensated, and communities retain control over their own language and culture. For personal data, the goal would be architectural safety — not just a privacy policy, but a system built so your data physically cannot be extracted.

The model is the AI "brain" — trained on enormous datasets to answer questions and take actions. Most powerful models are black boxes: they give you an answer but won't tell you why.

Open-source models would go beyond open weights (sharing the model output, not the recipe) to full transparency — the training data, the fine-tuning decisions, and the values baked in — so communities and countries could build or adapt models that reflect their own context and ethics.

Compute

Compute is the physical hardware — chips and data centres — that trains and runs AI models. Right now it's the choke point: access to specialized hardware determines who can train and deploy at scale, with a handful of hyperscalers controlling the cloud.

Open-source compute would mean more doors opening — through distributed and federated approaches, sovereign clouds, and idle GPUs finding productive use.
